## Design Context

### Users
University students (18-25) studying for exams, reviewing course material, and trying to understand concepts. They use this during late-night study sessions, before exams, and between classes. They want quick, trustworthy answers from their actual course materials — not generic AI output.

### Brand Personality
Clear, trustworthy, studious. A professional tool that earns trust through restraint and clarity — not through flashy decoration or forced warmth.

"Manhaj" means "curriculum" or "method" in Arabic. The design reflects that methodical, organized quality.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Tone**: Clean professional — the feel of a well-designed SaaS tool, not a toy or a chat gimmick
- **Colors**: Fixed brand palette (set by the logo, not open to reinterpretation): teal/green primary (`oklch(0.62 0.11 173)`), light blue secondary (`oklch(0.72 0.09 231)`), dark navy for text/emphasis (`oklch(0.28 0.06 258)`), white/near-white base. Neutrals tinted toward hue ~215-220 for cohesion. Interface stays mostly white/clean — brand colors are accents (10% rule), not a wash
- **Typography**: Archivo (display + UI chrome: headings, buttons, nav, badges) paired with Spectral (a screen-optimized serif, applied via `.font-reading` to prose — answers, chat content, landing copy). Avoids the Inter/Geist/Space Grotesk monoculture
- **Reference**: Notion, Linear — clean, structured, tool-oriented, professional
- **Anti-reference**: Generic AI chat interfaces (gradient text, rounded-2xl everything, emoji branding, cyan-on-dark). Also avoid Quizlet's aggressive blue or Anki's utilitarian gray
- **Theme**: Light-first, following the brand's white base. A gentle dark variant exists for late-night study, driven by `prefers-color-scheme`

### Design Principles
1. **Brand colors are load-bearing, not a starting suggestion** — teal/green + light blue + navy come from the logo itself. Don't drift toward a different accent hue for novelty's sake
2. **Content is king** — The interface gets out of the way. Answers and sources are the hero, not chrome
3. **No AI slop** — No identical card grids, no rounded-2xl on everything, no emoji branding, no generic spacing, no side-stripe borders. Every element should pass the "would a human designer do this?" test
4. **Structured but not rigid** — Notion-like organization with breathing room. Clean hierarchy without feeling clinical
5. **Reading-first** — Serif for prose, sans for UI chrome; spacing optimized for comprehension, not just aesthetics
